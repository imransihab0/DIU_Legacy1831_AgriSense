# Soil Types, Irrigation and Water Management — Bangladesh
Source: Compiled from SRDI (Soil Resource Development Institute) land & soil resource guides and BADC irrigation advisories — publicly available.

## Identifying soil type (farmer field test)
- Sandy: gritty, cannot form a ball when moist, water drains within minutes. Common in char (riverbed) land.
- Sandy loam: forms a weak ball that crumbles; slightly gritty.
- Loam: smooth, forms a ball and a short ribbon (<2.5 cm). The best all-purpose soil.
- Clay loam: forms ribbon 2.5-5 cm; slightly sticky when wet.
- Clay: sticky, forms long ribbon (>5 cm), cracks when dry, waterlogs in monsoon. Common in haor and beel areas.

## Crop suitability by soil
- Clay / clay loam: rice (boro, aman) is ideal — puddled clay holds standing water and cuts irrigation cost 20-30% vs loam. Poor for potato, onion, lentil (waterlogging, tuber deformation).
- Loam: suits almost everything — wheat, maize, potato, onion, tomato, mustard, jute.
- Sandy loam: excellent for potato, onion, groundnut (easy tuber/bulb expansion); rice on sandy loam needs 30-40% more irrigation water — usually uneconomic for boro.
- Sandy: only with heavy organic matter addition; suits watermelon, groundnut. High irrigation frequency, split fertilizer mandatory.

## Water availability classes and crop choice
- Full irrigation (own/rented shallow tubewell, STW): boro rice, potato, maize, onion all viable. Boro rice needs ~25-30 irrigations (~11,000 BDT/acre diesel STW); maize needs 4 (~6,000 BDT); wheat 2-3 (~4,500 BDT).
- Limited irrigation (shared/uncertain, 2-4 waterings possible): wheat, maize, mustard, winter vegetables. Avoid boro rice — a single missed irrigation at panicle initiation can cut yield 25%+.
- Rainfed only: aman rice (monsoon), lentil/mustard on residual moisture (sown right after aman harvest while soil is still moist), jute (pre-monsoon).

## Irrigation efficiency practices
- Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) for boro rice: irrigate only when water drops 15 cm below surface (use a perforated pipe); saves 4-6 irrigations (~1,500-2,500 BDT/acre) with no yield loss, and reduces BPH pressure.
- Irrigate in early morning or evening to cut evaporation loss ~20%.
- For potato/onion on sandy loam: frequent light irrigations beat occasional flooding; flooding cracks tubers.

## Salinity and drought notes
- Coastal (Khulna, Satkhira, Barguna): rabi soil salinity rises as dry season progresses — choose early short-duration varieties (BRRI dhan67 salt-tolerant; sunflower tolerates 6-8 dS/m).
- Barind tract (Rajshahi, Naogaon): groundwater declining; prioritize low-water crops (wheat, mustard, lentil, chickpea) over boro.
