## Soil pH status nationally (p.14, Table 1)
- Very Strongly to Strongly Acidic: 45.67% of arable land (3,921,994 ha)
- Slightly Acidic: 25.42% (2,182,698 ha)
- Neutral: 11.71% (1,005,558 ha)
- Slightly Alkaline: 17.00% (1,459,510 ha)
- Strongly Alkaline: 0.20% (17,039 ha); Very Strongly Alkaline: trace (65 ha)
- **Takeaway: ~71% of arable land is acidic (strongly + slightly).** Acidity limits P availability and nodulation in pulses; liming helps on strongly acidic soils.

## Soil organic matter (OM) status (p.15, Table 2)
- Very Low to Low: 34.83% (2,990,355 ha)
- Medium: 59.19% (5,082,396 ha)
- High: 4.58% (393,512 ha); Very High: 1.40% (120,601 ha)
- **Takeaway: ~35% of land is low in OM → organic manure/compost strongly justified; FRG lists OF (organic fertilizer) rates per crop.**

## Phosphorus (P) status — upland crops (p.16, Table 3)
- Very Low to Low: 56.16% (4,822,353 ha)
- Medium: 20.75% (1,781,569 ha)
- Optimum: 10.33% (887,376 ha)
- High to Very High: 12.76% (109,566 ha)
- **Takeaway: >56% of upland soils are low in P → phosphorus (TSP) is usually the binding nutrient for upland crops.**

## Phosphorus (P) status — wetland rice (p.18, Table 4)
- Very Low to Low: 50.27% (4,316,455 ha)
- Medium: 21.43% (1,840,200 ha)
- Optimum: 12.38% (1,063,152 ha)
- High to Very High: 15.92% (1,367,056 ha)
- Rice soils are slightly less P-deficient than upland, but half are still low.

## Potassium (K) status — upland crops (p.20, Table 5)
- Very Low to Low: 59.85% (5,139,339 ha)
- Medium: 23.51% (2,018,590 ha)
- Optimum: 9.37% (805,010 ha)
- High to Very High: 7.27% (623,925 ha)
- **Takeaway: ~60% of upland soils are low in K → MoP is broadly needed; K also aids disease/lodging resistance.**

## Soil texture and practical suitability
- The atlas maps loamy-to-clayey soils separately for upland vs wetland-rice crops (P and K status tables are split by these two land-use types, p.16–23).
- Loamy/clayey soils dominate the fertility-mapped arable land; these retain nutrients and water well and suit rice (wetland) and most upland crops.
- FRG advises: on **light-textured (sandy) soils**, split potassium — half basal, half with the last N topdressing (see FRG rice note) — because K leaches faster on sandy soils.

## How to use this atlas with FRG
- SRDI status class (Low/Medium/Optimum/High) maps onto the FRG soil-fertility level that selects the dose.
- Default assumption absent a soil test: most Bangladeshi arable land is acidic, low-to-medium OM, low in P and K → use the FRG **Medium (often Low)** dose for P and K, add organic matter, and consider lime on strongly acidic plots.

## Data provenance / method
- Maps built by Digital Soil Mapping (spatial interpolation IDW + regression, reclassification, validation) from SRDI soil legacy data and Upazila Nirdeshika surveys (p.12).
- First national soil fertility maps 1998; updated 2010; this edition 2020 (p.9 Prologue). Upazila-level detail lives in the "Upazila Nirdeshika" land & soil guides (not in this atlas).
