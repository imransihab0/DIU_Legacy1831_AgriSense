## 1. Introduction and Overview

### 🌾 Importance of Rice in Bangladesh
- Rice is the staple food crop of Bangladesh
- Bangladesh's economy and culture are deeply intertwined with rice
- Population continues to grow while agricultural land decreases due to:
  - Housing construction
  - Industries/factories
  - Markets and roads
  - River erosion

### ⚠️ Challenges in Rice Production
Natural disasters affecting rice cultivation:
- Drought
- Floods
- Tidal surges
- Salinity
- Cold wave
- Cyclones
- Hailstorms

### 🌍 Global Rice Production Context
- **Bangladesh ranks 4th** among rice-producing countries
- Average yield: **4.5 tons/hectare**
- China, Japan, and Korea: **6-6.5 tons/hectare**
- Bangladesh advantage: **Three crops per year** on same land

### 📅 Historical Development
- **1968**: First high-yielding variety (IRRI) introduced from IRRI
- First HYV "IRRI" yielded **5-6 tons/hectare** (18-21 maunds)
- **1970**: Bangladesh Rice Research Institute (BRRI) established
- BRRI develops season-specific and environment-adapted HYVs
- Current BRRI varieties cover **80%** of rice area and contribute **91%** of total production

## 2. High-Yielding and Modern Rice Varieties

### 🔬 Characteristics of High-Yielding Rice
- Strong and sturdy stem
- Erect leaves
- Remains green even after ripening
- Traditional local varieties have weak stems, drooping leaves, low tillering capacity

### 🏷️ Modern Variety Classification

**BRRI varieties naming system:**
- Initially: BR1 to BR26 (with local names)
- After 1993: BRRI dhan27 onwards
- Total varieties developed: **102** (93 inbred, 7 hybrid)

**Difference between Inbred and Hybrid:**
- **Inbred**: Seeds can be saved for next planting
- **Hybrid**: Seeds cannot be saved

### 📋 Variety Suitability by Season
- Aus
- Aman
- Boro

## 4. Seedbed (Nursery) Preparation

### 🏗️ Standard Seedbed Preparation
- Size: **1 meter wide**
- **40-50 cm** space between beds
- Make beds using soil from both sides
- Level top with wooden plank or bamboo
- Sow seeds **3-4 hours** after bed preparation
- Drainage channels between beds essential

### 🌊 Floating Seedbed Method
**Purpose:** Alternative method for flood-prone areas or when water recedes late

**Materials needed:**
- Bamboo mats
- Banana stems/rafts
- 2-3 cm thick layer of decomposed organic matter

**Process:**
1. Construct floating platform on floodwater
2. Sow germinated seeds
3. Tie the platform to bamboo poles to prevent floating away
4. No need for manual watering

### 📦 Dapog Seedbed Method

**Process:**
- Prepare on cemented floor or raised platform
- Materials: bamboo, bricks, or banana stems to create structure
- Spread polythene or banana leaves (with mid-rib removed)
- Sow pre-germinated seeds densely
- Rate: **1 kg seeds per 1 square meter**
- Press lightly with hand or piece of wood (2-3 times daily)

**Care and Use:**
- Water every **5-6 hours** so roots stay in contact with water
- Ideal for areas with limited water access
- **30-40 square meters** of dapog seedbed can transplant **1 hectare**
- Ready in **14 days**

**Advantages:**
- Good quality seedlings
- Strong root system
- No weed problem in main field
- Longer lifespan in field

## 7. Alternative Cultivation Method: Split Transplanting (ফিকেড়াপণ)

### 📍 Description
- Particularly useful in **Rangpur region** (called "Kavalan")
- In **Jamalpur region** (called "Gachh")

### 🔄 Process
1. Transplant densely at **10 × 10 cm** spacing (5-6 seedlings/hill)
2. After **25-40 days**, uproot every alternate row
3. Leave one row and remove two plants from remaining rows
4. Final spacing: **20 × 20 cm**

### ✅ Advantages
- Matures **7-10 days earlier** than normal transplanting
- **10-15% higher yield**
- Can plant photo-insensitive varieties in the field
- Can be done even in waterlogged conditions
- Reduces lodging risk

**Recommended varieties:**
- Boro: **BRRI dhan29**
- Aman: **BRRI dhan49**

## 8. Fertilizer Management

### 📋 General Principles
Adjust fertilizer based on:
- Weather conditions
- Soil fertility
- Variety type
- Life duration
- Expected yield

### ⏰ Fertilizer Application Timing

**Urea application:**
- **First application**: After establishment
- **Second application**: At maximum tillering
- **Third application**: At panicle initiation

### 🧪 Nutrient Deficiency Management

**Sulfur Deficiency Symptoms:**
- Yellowing of young leaves
- Delayed maturity
- Small panicles

**Remedial measure:** Apply **8 kg gypsum/acre** after draining water

**Zinc Deficiency Symptoms:**
- Yellow-brown spots on old leaves
- Reduced tillering
- White or brown patches

**Remedial measure:** Apply **1.5 kg zinc sulfate/acre**

### 🏞️ Soil Fertility Classification

**Low fertility:**
- Boro: <1.0-1.5 tons/ha without fertilizer
- Aman: <2.0-2.5 tons/ha without fertilizer

**Medium fertility:**
- Boro: 3.0-3.5 tons/ha without fertilizer
- Aman: 3.5 tons/ha without fertilizer

**High fertility:**
- Boro: 4.0 tons/ha without fertilizer
- Aman: 3.5-4.0 tons/ha without fertilizer

## 9. Leaf Color Chart (LCC) for Nitrogen Management

### 🎨 Description
- Four-color plastic chart
- Compares leaf color to determine urea need
- Saves **20-25%** urea fertilizer

### 📝 Method
1. Select 10 hills/plants
2. From each hill, select topmost fully opened leaf
3. Place LCC on middle portion of leaf
4. Compare color with chart
5. Take average if color varies

### 📊 Application Guidelines (Table 8)

| Parameter | Aus | Aman | Boro | Transplanted Aman | Transplanted Boro |
|-----------|-----|------|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| LCC Reading Below | 3.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| Measurement Start | 15 days after transplanting | 15 days after sowing | 15 days after transplanting | 21 days after transplanting | 25 days after sowing |
| Measurement Frequency | 30 days | 10 days | 10 days | 10 days | 10 days |
| Urea Amount | 7.5 kg/ha | 9 kg/ha | - | - | - |

## 10. Weed Management

### ⚠️ Importance
- Weeds compete for light, water, nutrients
- Harbor pests and diseases
- **Aus season** (especially broadcast Aus) has highest weed pressure

### ⏰ Critical Periods

| Season | Days Weed-Free Required |
|--------|------------------------|
| Aus | 30-40 days |
| Aman | 30-40 days |
| Boro | 40-50 days |

### 🛠️ Methods of Weed Control

#### 1. Manual Weeding
- First weeding: **15 days** after transplanting
- Second weeding: **30-35 days** after transplanting

#### 2. Mechanical Weeding

## 11. Water Management

### 💧 Irrigation Statistics in Bangladesh
- **60%** of agricultural land is irrigated
- **15%** using surface water
- **85%** using groundwater
- Deep tubewells: **~36,000**
- Shallow tubewells: **~1.6 million**

### 🚰 PIPED WATER DISTRIBUTION TECHNOLOGY (BRRI Developed)

**Features:**
- 25-30% water loss in traditional canals
- Use PVC pipes with T-joints, bends, caps
- Reduces water conveyance loss to **near zero**
- Saves **31.6%** time
- Can distribute water to high and low areas
- **30% more irrigation coverage** possible

### 🔄 Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)

**Method:**
- Install PVC pipes **7-10 cm** diameter
- **25 cm** long with holes
- Placed **15 cm** apart
- Apply irrigation **10-15 days** after transplanting
- Maintain alternate wetting and drying
- Saves **30% water**
- Reduces irrigation costs
- Increases water use efficiency

## 12. Pest and Disease Management

### 🐛 Major Rice Pests

#### 1. Leaf Folder (পাতামোড়ানো পোকা)
**Symptoms:**
- Leaves folded and fed upon inside
- White streaks on leaves

**Management:**
- Use light traps
- Apply recommended insecticides when 25% leaves affected (Table 12)

#### 2. Rice Caseworm (চুশি পোকা)
**Symptoms:**
- Cuts leaf tips
- Makes tubular cases
- Floats on water surface

**Management:**
- Light traps
- Remove with hand nets
- Drain water and dry soil
- Apply insecticide when 25% leaves damaged

#### 3. Swarming Caterpillar (েলো পোকা)
**Symptoms:**
- Eats leaves from edges
- Entire field can be defoliated
- Common in low-lying areas

**Management:**
- Plow after harvest
- Light traps
- Encourage birds
- Apply insecticide at 25% leaf damage

#### 4. Grasshopper (খাসফলিৰ)
**Symptoms:**
- Eats leaf margins
- Can swarm in large numbers

**Management:**
- Hand nets
- Encourage birds
- Apply insecticide at 25% leaf damage

#### 5. Long-horned Cricket (দীঘশ্বাঙ্গী উরচুলা)
**Symptoms:**
- Chews leaves leaving only veins
- Damaged leaves appear dried

**Management:**
- Encourage birds
- Light traps
- Apply insecticide at 25% leaf damage

#### 6. Green Leafhopper (সবুজ পাতাফুিঙ)
**Symptoms:**
- Sucks sap from leaves
- Stunts plant growth
- Transmits tungro virus

**Management:**
- Light traps
- Apply insecticide if 1 hopper/plant in nursery or field with tungro symptoms

#### 7. Brown Planthopper (বাদামি গাছফুিঙ) - ⚠️ CRITICAL
**Symptoms:**
- Sucks sap from stems
- Causes "hopper burn" (yellowing and drying)
- Can destroy entire field

**Management:**
- Monitor regularly (Boro: March-April, Aman: August)
- Light traps
- Wider spacing (25×15 or 20×20 cm)
- Avoid excessive urea
- Drain water and dry soil for 5-6 days
- Use resistant varieties
- Apply insecticide with water jet
- **⚠️ AVOID:** Synthetic pyrethroids (cypermethrin, deltamethrin) as they increase BPH population

#### 8. White-backed Planthopper (সাদা-পিঠ গাছফুিঙ)
**Symptoms:** Similar to BPH
**Management:** Same as BPH

#### 9. Mealy Bug (ছাতৰা পোকা)
**Symptoms:**
- White cottony mass at stem nodes
- Severe attack prevents panicle emergence
- Common in dry weather

**Management:**
- Uproot and destroy affected plants
- Apply insecticide (Table 12)

#### 10. Thrips (িপস)
**Symptoms:**
- Scrapes leaf surface
- Sucks sap
- Leaves curl and become silvery

**Management:**
- Proper irrigation and urea application
- Apply insecticide if heavy attack (Table 12)

#### 11. Rice Bug (পািক পোকা)
**Symptoms:**
- Attacks at milk stage
- White spots on grains
- Bad odor from bugs

**Management:**
- Light traps
- Spray insecticide if 2 bugs/hill (Table 12)
- Spray at heading stage

#### 12. Ear Cutting Caterpillar (শীষ কাটা েলো পোকা)
**Symptoms:**
- Cuts leaves and panicles
- Attacks at night

**Management:**
- Plow after harvest
- Encourage birds
- Apply irrigation to drown caterpillars

### 🦠 Major Rice Diseases

#### 1. Blast (ব্লাস্ট)

**Symptoms:**
- **Leaves:** Spindle-shaped spots with gray center and brown border
- **Nodes:** Blackish lesions
- **Panicles:** Infected at neck causing grain shattering
- **Seedling blight** in nursery

**Favorable Conditions:**
- Cool, cloudy weather
- Excessive nitrogen
- Dense planting

**Management:**
- Use resistant varieties
- Balanced fertilizer
- Avoid excessive urea
- Seed treatment (Table 13)
- Apply fungicide at heading (Table 12)
- **Varieties at risk:** BRRI dhan28, dhan29, dhan50, dhan63, dhan64, dhan81, all aromatic and hybrid varieties

**Blast Management Table:**

| Disease | Fungicide | Rate (per hectare) |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| Blast | Tilt 250 EC | 500 ml |
| Blast | Score 250 EC | 500 ml |
| Blast | Folicur 250 EC | 500 ml |
| Blast | Nativo 75 WG | 400 g |
| Blast | Indofil M-45 | 2.0 kg |

#### 2. Sheath Blight (মখালমপাতা)

**Symptoms:**
- Initially grayish lesions on sheath
- Later, oval to irregular spots
- Spots enlarge and join
- Leaves may die

**Favorable Conditions:**
- High humidity
- Excessive nitrogen
- Dense planting

**Management:**
- Remove diseased plants
- Balanced fertilizer especially potash
- Apply recommended fungicide
- Spray on lower parts of plants

#### 3. Bakanae (বাকান)

**Symptoms:**
- Infected seedlings: Taller and paler
- Thin, pale yellow-green stems
- Adventitious roots on lower nodes
- Plants gradually die

**Management:**
- Remove infected plants
- Seed treatment: Carbendazim (1 liter water + 3 g) for 10-12 hours
- Seed treatment gives best control

#### 4. Bacterial Leaf Blight (পাতা পেড়া)

**Symptoms:**
- Water-soaked lesions on leaf tips
- Turn yellow then white
- Can cause complete drying

**Management:**
- Use resistant varieties
- Balanced fertilizer (especially potash)
- Drain water and dry field

#### 5. Bacterial Leaf Streak (পাতা লালচে রেখা)

**Symptoms:**
- Translucent, water-soaked streaks
- Streaks enlarge lengthwise
- Yellow-brown, linear lesions
- More severe in warm weather

**Management:**
- Hot water seed treatment (55°C for 15 min)
- 60 kg potash + 60 kg DAP in 10L water
- Apply to affected fields

#### 6. Sheath Rot or Sheath Blight (চারপেড়া বা সদসনেদা)

**Symptoms:**
- Seeds rot in nursery
- Seedlings die before emergence
- Infected seedlings at base become soft and rot
- Irregular patches in nursery

**Favorable Conditions:**
- Waterlogged soil
- Use of diseased seed
- Weed infestation
- Decomposing organic matter

**Management:**
- Use clean seeds
- Proper drainage
- Avoid waterlogging
- Remove infected seedlings

## 13. Harvesting, Threshing and Storage

### 🔍 Harvesting Indicators
- **80-90%** of grains turn golden yellow
- Grains become hard and firm

### ⚠️ Harvesting Precautions
- Avoid over-ripening (shattering loss)
- Prevent bird damage
- Keep field dry
- Harvest early to prevent grain loss

### 🌾 Threshing
- Thresh immediately after harvesting
- Use clean threshing floor
- Spread tarpaulin or polythene sheets

### ☀️ Drying
- Dry for **4-5 days** after threshing
- Ensure moisture content **≤12%**
- Kernel should make "click" sound when bitten

### 🏠 Seed Storage Guidelines

**Container Types:**
- Earthenware pots
- Brick/concrete bins
- Plastic drums

**Storage Conditions:**
- Clean and dry containers
- Treat with insecticides (0.25 kg per ton seed)
- Store in cool, dry place
- Keep containers off floor
- Seal containers properly
- Use fumigation if needed

## 14. Yield Gap Analysis

### 📊 Potential vs. Actual Yield

| Variety | Potential Yield | National Average |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|
| BRRI dhan28 (Boro) | 6.5 tons/ha | 4.2 tons/ha |
| BRRI dhan29 (Boro) | 9-10 tons/ha | 4.2 tons/ha |

### ❌ Reasons for Yield Gap

**1. Knowledge-based:**
- Poor quality seed
- Incorrect fertilizer application
- Improper water management

**2. Management-based:**
- Wrong seedling age
- Incorrect transplanting time
- Delayed fertilizer application

**3. Economic/Resource-based:**
- Limited access to inputs
- Inability to adopt recommended practices

### ✅ Strategies to Close Yield Gap

| Strategy | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Variety selection | Choose suitable varieties for specific areas |
| Quality seed | Use certified, pure, healthy seed |
| Quality seedlings | Proper nursery management |
| Land preparation | Level field, weed-free |
| Fertilizer management | Balanced and timely application |
| Water management | Maintain proper irrigation |
| Pest management | Integrated Pest Management |
| Harvesting | Timely harvest and proper post-harvest management |

**💰 Income potential:** Good management can yield profit of **2,500 Taka per bigha**

## 15. Hybrid Rice Cultivation

### 🌱 Seedbed Preparation
- Same as HYV methods
- Organic fertilizer: **2 tons** cow dung per hectare
- Additional fertilizers: **4 kg TSP, 7 kg MoP**
- After 10 days: **7 kg urea + 10 kg gypsum**
- Extra urea (4-5 kg) if nitrogen deficiency persists

### 🌾 Transplanting
- **20-25 day** old seedlings
- Spacing: **20 × 20 cm**
- **2-3** seedlings/hill

### 💊 Fertilizer Management
- Based on soil fertility
- Higher nitrogen requirement than HYVs

### 💧 Water Management
- Maintain **5-7 cm** water
- Increase water level during tillering

### 🌿 Weed Management
- Keep field weed-free for first **40-50 days**

## 16. Haor Area Cultivation

### 🌊 Challenges
- Flash floods submerge mature crops
- Flooding starts in **3rd week of September**

### ✅ Strategies
- Select suitable varieties (BRRI dhan29, BRRI dhan49)
- Early sowing
- **40-45 day** old seedlings
- BRRI dhan49 is early maturing with similar yield

## 17. Machinery and Equipment

### 🔧 1. BRRI Urea Super Granule (USG) Applicator
- Places urea granules **6-8 cm** deep
- One-time application per season
- Covers **1.0-1.5 bighas/day**
- Saves **25-30%** urea
- Cost: **6,000 Taka**

### 🔧 2. BRRI Deep Placement Fertilizer Applicator
- Weight: **7.5 kg**
- Places fertilizer **6-8 cm** deep
- One laborer can operate
- Field needs **0.5 cm** standing water
- Cost: **6,000 Taka**

### 🔧 3. BRRI Power Weeder
- Two-row weeder
- Operated by one person
- Covers **8-10 decimal land/hour**
- Cost: **450 Taka**

### 🔧 4. BRRI Self-Propelled Rice-Wheat Reaper
- **1.2 meter** cutting width
- **0.5-0.7 L/hour** fuel consumption
- Cuts **1 bigha in 4-5 hours**
- Easy to operate

### 🔧 5. BRRI Rice-Wheat Reaper (Powered by Power Tiller)
- Attaches to power tiller
- Covers **1.0-1.5 bighas/hour**
- Works in standing crop conditions
- Cost: **40,000 Taka** (excluding attachment parts)

### 🔧 6. BRRI Combine Thresher
- Motor/electric powered
- Preserves straw
- Multiple workers can operate

### 🔧 7. BRRI Rice-Wheat Thresher
**Models:**
- **CH-7**: 18 maunds rice or 10 maunds wheat/hour
- **CH-8**: 25 maunds rice or 15 maunds wheat/hour
- Motor/electric powered

### 🔧 8. BRRI Dryer
- Capacity: **200-350 kg**
- Drying time: **7-10 hours**
- Motor: **0.5 HP**
- Cost: **15,000 Taka**

### 🔧 9. BRRI Chopper
- Cuts straw for: Animal feed, mulching, bed preparation, compost
- Power: **4 HP** diesel or **2 HP** electric
- Cost: **45,000 Taka**

### 🔧 10. BRRI Air-Blast Rice Mill
- Motor: **15 HP** electric or **20 HP** diesel
- Uses #8 huller
- Capacity: **300-400 kg/hour**
- **1-2% more** head rice
- Cost: **100,000 Taka**

### 🔧 11. BRRI Power Winnower
- **0.5 HP** motor
- Two laborers required
- **10 times** faster than traditional winnowing
