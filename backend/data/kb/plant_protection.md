## Rice — pests & diseases (p.7–8)
- **Seven major insect pests and seven major diseases are most prevalent in rice; Brown planthopper (BPH) and Stem borer have the highest incidence (p.8).**
- Key insect pests: Brown planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens); Green leafhopper (Nephotettix nigropictus, N. virescens) — a Tungro virus vector; Rice hispa (Dicladispa armigera); Yellow stem borer (Scirpophaga incertulas, Sesamia inferens, Chilo polychrysus); Swarming caterpillar (Spodoptera mauritia); Leaf roller/folder (Cnaphalocrocis medinalis); Ear-cutting caterpillar (Mythimna separata).
- Key diseases: Blast (Pyricularia oryzae); Sheath blight (Rhizoctonia solani); Sheath rot (Sarocladium oryzae); Stem rot (Sclerotium oryzae); Bacterial leaf blight/BLB (Xanthomonas campestris pv. oryzae); Tungro (virus); Ufra (Ditylenchus angustus, a nematode).

## Rice — resistant varieties & IPM tools (p.12)
- **BRRI dhan26, 31, 35 = resistant to Brown planthopper (BPH).**
- **BRRI dhan28, 45 = resistant to Blast.**
- **BRRI dhan37, 41, 42 = resistant to Tungro virus.**
- **BRRI dhan32, 44, 45 = resistant to Blast & Sheath blight.**
- For yellow stem borer, **pheromone sleeve traps** are an effective IPM tool.
- Recommending a resistant variety is the cheapest first-line pest control the agent can suggest.

## Jute — pests & diseases (p.7–8)
- Insects: Jute hairy caterpillar (Spilosoma obliqua); Jute semilooper (Anomis sabulifera); Jute stem weevil (Apion corchori); White mite (Polyphagotarsonemus latus); Cricket (Tarbinskiellus portentosus).
- Diseases: Stem rot (Macrophomina phaseolina); Black band (Botryodiplodia theobromae); Anthracnose (Colletotrichum corchori); Dieback (Glomerella cingulata); Root knot (Meloidogyne incognita, M. javanica); Chlorosis (virus).

## Wheat — pests & diseases (p.7–8)
- Insects: Cutworm (Agrotis spp.); Termites (Odontotermes obesus); Aphid (Schizaphis graminum).
- Diseases: Leaf blight (Bipolaris sorokiniana — the major BD wheat disease); Rust (Puccinia recondita); Loose smut (Ustilago tritici).

## Sugarcane — pests & diseases (p.7–8)
- Insects: Top shoot borer (Scirpophaga excerptalis); Root borer (Emmalocera depressella); Leafhopper (Pyrilla perpusilla); Termite (Odontotermes obesus); White grub (Holotrichia spp.); Woolly aphid (Rhopalosiphum padi).
- Diseases: Red rot (Colletotrichum falcatum); White leaf (Mycoplasma); Wilt (Cephalosporium saccharum).

## Potato & vegetables — pests & diseases (p.7–9)
- Potato insects: Cutworm (Agrotis ipsilon) and potato tuber pests.
- Oilseed & vegetable emerging disease: **White mould (Sclerotinia sclerotiorum)** — serious soil- and seed-borne pathogen, reported as a newer disease over recent years (p.9).
- (Note: potato late blight — the classic weather-driven vegetable disease — is not detailed here; pull it from BARI/DAE leaflets. It rises at ~10–20°C with high humidity.)

## Fruit crop pests (p.8)
- Mango: leaf defoliator (Cricula trifenestrata), leaf gall psyllid (Apsylla cistellata), orange fruit fly (Chaetodacus spp.).
- Citrus: psyllid bug (Euphalerus citrii — vector context), mealybug (Pseudococcus vigatus).
- Banana beetle (Nodostoma viridipennis); Coconut rhinoceros beetle (Oryctes rhinoceros) & red palm weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus).

## IPM principles & program (p.9–12)
- IPM began in rice in 1981 (FAO Inter-Country Program); expanded via **Farmer Field Schools (FFS)** run by DAE and NGOs.
- IPM combines **cultural, mechanical, physical, biological, and chemical measures** — chemical as one component, not the default.
- Biological control: private companies rear and market **parasitoids and predators**.
- Approach for the agent: prefer resistant variety + cultural/biological controls first; chemical control as last resort at economic threshold.

## Pesticide management & safety (p.12–22)
- Bangladesh **imports and formulates** pesticides (no local active-ingredient manufacture); ~75 registered companies, 8 multinational.
- Registered categories include miticides, insecticides, fungicides, rodenticides (e.g. Zinc phosphide, Bromadiolone) etc. — the report lists registered generic names/product counts, not field doses.
- Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) increasingly promoted, especially for export crops (p.22).
- **Do not invent chemical names or doses; only registered generics are named here.** Actual application rates come from DAE/BRRI crop-protection leaflets.
